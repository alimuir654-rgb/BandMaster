# Band experiences and continuity

Owner feedback, 2 November 2026 evening: the game still feels like a collection of screens and buttons. Meaningful activities need anticipation, a sense of place, interaction with the musicians, transitions and aftermath. Travelling to a gig should show the journey; auditions and songwriting need substantially more involvement than short notes or three choices. This is the next-development brief, not a claim that V8 has been repaired.

## Design requirement

Treat a whole band experience as the design and acceptance unit. Every important stage needs a place, the people involved, something happening and a reason to continue. Images, restrained scene motion, framing, fades and character responses establish continuity. Keep the band, its songs, the venue, the journey and the consequences consistent across those stages.

Ordinary navigation stays responsive. Use deliberate pacing for meaningful first visits, decisions, performances and reactions. Avoid turning every routine action into a compulsory cutscene or adding an extra Continue screen for every paragraph. A familiar visit can be shorter once the player understands it. Reduced motion and skip-to-settled-frame controls preserve the same information, choices and saved state.

## First implementation unit: one complete gig

Build and inspect one venue's whole experience before multiplying it across the game.

| Moment | What the player experiences | Interaction and continuity |
|---|---|---|
| Set out | Meet the band at the vehicle; show the actual destination, time of day and transport context. | Carry the selected lineup, set and preparation forward. Do not invent an owned van: the current journey records a local lift. |
| Travel | Vehicle/road imagery with restrained movement and a transition that gives a sense of leaving one place and reaching another. | A relevant band exchange can reflect actual preparation or pressure. Travel presentation never spends another day or charges twice. |
| Arrive and load in | Venue exterior gives way to the same venue's interior; the band gets its equipment into place. | Establish who is present and what is happening before offering the next important action. |
| Soundcheck | See the room and the band preparing, then use the existing physical controls. | Let the player witness a meaningful response to the committed plan; do not expose hidden quality/reach formulas. |
| Backstage | A brief moment with the actual musicians before the set. | Earn a conversation from preparation, fatigue, commitments or relationships. Avoid a mandatory random dilemma on every gig. |
| Take the stage | Hold the moment as backstage gives way to the audience-facing stage and the band takes its place. | The transition builds anticipation; the saved current set starts only through explicit input. |
| Perform the set | Selected finished songs progress in their saved order, with actual completed songs crossed out and an honest modelled crowd response. | One bounded consequential interaction in the first pilot. Musicians shown with instruments need proper approved performance sources; do not present a moving cutout as playing animation. |
| Come offstage | Rejoin the band after the set; let a reaction land before the full receipt. | Show actual consequences from the one saved settlement. Carry relevant facts into the contact/next-goal loop. |
| Pack up and leave | A short packing/loading/departure sequence closes the night and leads naturally to the next day or next goal. | Acknowledge what happened and what the band is taking away. Do not grant another reward or charge another journey merely for watching it. |

The arrival, soundcheck and stage systems already exist, but their current three-phase structure is not this complete experience. ShowFlow has a travel acknowledgement and whole-show summaries; ShowReplay occurs after settlement. It is not a live per-song performance cursor. The performance pilot still requires its complete additive booking/set/snapshot/cursor/choice/save contract before source work. Do not disguise replay as unresolved gameplay.

## Auditions: meeting and trying out a musician

The musician arrives in a recognisable rehearsal setting, introduces themself and takes part in a short role-specific try-out. Give the player a purposeful response: ask them to try a different approach, respond to what happened or discuss how they work with others. Let the musician react in character before the invitation decision. Their contribution, working style, ambition and availability should become understandable through the encounter.

Retain the actual identity and musical biography, the original talent budget and the existing free-audition/no-day contract unless a separate economy change is approved. A larger text report is not sufficient acceptance. Audition performance/audio needs real cleared material and appropriate visuals; never offer a pretend playback button or imply instrument animation/audio that does not exist.

## Songwriting: developing something with the band

Begin with an idea from an actual bandmate and the current saved song. Let the player work through a meaningful creative exchange: try an approach, encounter a musician's suggestion or disagreement, revise a fragment/arrangement, and finish with a recognisable piece of progress. Unfinished ideas remain available when the band returns. Later rehearsals, recordings and gigs should recognise the same song and relevant contributions.

Keep the three-session writing structure as the current underlying contract while replacing its shallow presentation/interaction in a separately scoped pilot. Every future creative choice needs an actual represented change or response; new buttons alone do not supply musical substance. Real musical material requires an explicit asset/source plan. Keep composition distinct from gear condition, retain original song identity/snapshots/costs/day rules and do not award absent members invented contributions.

## Promoter/agent contact and radio

Carry the same arrival, interaction, reaction and departure pattern into meaningful meetings. Use an existing actual promoter/contact; an agent relationship must be implemented before the game treats one as hired. Radio already saves held questions/answers and charges at its final wrap. Improve the sense of entering a student station, meeting the presenter, going on air and leaving without re-running or charging the completed interview. Routine later appearances should not repeat the entire first-visit introduction unnecessarily.

## Production and acceptance

Plan the visual sequence before coding: required independent environment/performer/vehicle layers, camera direction, focal crops and native text/control areas. Reuse suitable verified assets with honest provenance. The approved poster is not a performance layer; the audience-side stage and reverse crowd view are different cameras. A04's headstock/edge failure and missing bass/drum performance sources remain held. New artwork proposals retain the owner's approval gate; no generation is authorised merely to fill a stage.

The implementation should use a small activity presentation coordinator with explicit owned phases, not a wholesale dialogue-engine rewrite. Save validated gameplay checkpoints at meaningful explicit actions. Tweens, fades, resize, skip, reduced motion and reload cannot resolve a model, choose an answer, complete a song or spend again. Preserve the original transaction owner, terminal/radio/due-show priorities, whole-career save-failure rollback and legacy receipts. Define the exact settlement boundary before code; current live performance already applies several post-systems.

Acceptance is an actual native playthrough from preparation to packing up, reopen/resume at important boundaries, one real failed-save/retry, and four-size original-image review. Judge rhythm, anticipation, musician presence and the remembered outcome. Keep technical correctness, internal visual judgement and the owner's experience acceptance separate. Source implementation, new art and a replacement APK remain future work after this brief.
